Idea 1 of 4

Less linear algebra

The noise needs w = A⁻ᴴc and then A⁻¹Qw. Since cᴴA⁻¹ = wᴴ:

noise ∝ Re(wᴴ Q w)

so the third solve is redundant: two solves, not three.

The other two share one sparse LU (SciPy SuperLU) on the fixed 2,597-entry pattern.

Iterative refinement keeps it accurate on hard setups such as Voyager.

Per factorisation, one 751 × 751 complex system

Dense LU

36 ms

Sparse LU

0.6 ms

60× cheaper

Forward simulation 14.7 s → 0.20 s

Idea one: do less linear algebra. The noise formula can be rewritten as the real part of w-Hermitian Q w, so the third solve per frequency is redundant. The other two share one sparse LU factorisation on the fixed pattern, 60 times cheaper than dense. Iterative refinement keeps hard setups like Voyager accurate. The forward run fell from 14.7 seconds to 0.2.

Idea 2 of 4

A hand-written gradient (adjoint method)

For A x = b and the incoming gradient x̄:

λ = A⁻ᵀ x̄

same LU, one solve

b̄ = λ

Ā[k] = −λ[row_k] · x[col_k]

non-zeros only

Checked against JAX’s own autodiff. It is the plain transpose, not the conjugate one: JAX uses the plain transpose for holomorphic maps.

Effect

We still save residuals for the reverse pass, but only what the gradient needs (inputs, factorisations, solutions) instead of the 9 GB tape.

No full 751 × 751 gradient matrix per frequency: all 193 gradients are formed on the non-zeros only.

Gradient 3.4 s → 0.12 s

Idea two: write the gradient by hand with the adjoint method. One extra solve with the transposed matrix, reusing the same LU factors, gives lambda; the matrix gradient is then only needed on the non-zero entries. We checked it against JAX's own autodiff. Instead of a 9 gigabyte tape we keep only inputs, factors and solutions, and the gradient went from 3.4 seconds to 0.12.

Idea 3 of 4

No physics left for XLA to compile

The whole simulation runs as NumPy / SciPy code behind a single jax.custom_vjp.

Same physics source: the formulas in components.py are reused unchanged, re-bound to NumPy.

Derivatives come from forward-mode dual numbers running that same code: exact, no finite differences.

XLA sees only two callbacks.

What we learned

Three habits that made it work

1

Profile before optimising. After the solver was fixed, compiling was 90 % of the time, not the linear algebra.

2

Exploit the structure. A fixed sparsity pattern means: sparse, factor once, differentiate on the non-zeros.

3

Keep one source of truth, and check every gradient. The same physics code runs on NumPy and dual numbers; all 193 gradients are tested, not one.
